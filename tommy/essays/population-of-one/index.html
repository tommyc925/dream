Population of One

Tommy · Ticket № 01

I once dreamed I was swimming through a flooded city – through crystal-clear water in a library with fish swimming around me. It was magical. I’ve had nightmares, I’ve had dreams I thought were real, dreams I wish were real, and dreams I don’t even have words to explain.

One moment I’m in a world where the only law of physics is the limit of my imagination. The next I’m in a world where few laws are my own. Most of the time the transition is a sunrise. Sometimes it’s the end of a walk.

Dreams are lonely. My brother Shaun was in that library dream. I remember his face when he got pushed into the water and was swimming with a whale. His look of shock was so funny. When I woke up, the magical world only existed for me. Population of one.

That brief visit to a magical world was beautiful, but if I had to choose, I’d pick the real Shaun every time. Luckily we never had to choose.

Shaun exists. Whales and fish exist. Libraries exist. The combination is definitely strange, but it’s just a shuffling of real things. I probably shouldn’t flood my public library, but that dream left me appreciating the real Shaun more and may have inspired a little more whimsy in my life.

We all carry around worlds that don’t exist. Some just for fun, some dreaded conversations we’ve had 20 times in our head, and some lives we wish we lived. Sometimes we imagine to escape the world. Sometimes we imagine ways to shape it.

What is the difference between a dream and a plan? Tell someone you dream of opening a bakery or that you plan on opening one, and they’ll hear two different things. Both bakeries only exist in your head.

Not every dream needs to become a plan. Maybe you love baking and find bakeries charming, but don’t want to wake up at 4am every day to bake bread. Enjoy the dream.

What about the persistent dreams? A former boss called them the dreams that won’t quit. I have one. She has one. I bet a lot of people do.

My dream grew over 20 years. I learned how to code, picked jobs that moved me in that direction, and had a few false starts. One of the hardest parts was figuring out how to explain the world in my head. Another was the fear that if I finally got someone to see it, they wouldn’t care.

But I don’t think that’s what happened. Sometimes I wasn’t very good at explaining myself. Other times, a conversation revealed something about my dream that I hadn’t figured out yet. Sharing a dream isn’t just about finding people who believe in it. Sometimes it’s how you discover what you’re really trying to build.

Explaining my dream is also really scary. My dream is technical, but if it’s such a good idea, why hasn’t someone smarter already built it? If my idea is unique, what if I’m just not talented enough to build it? The dream is beautiful. I can’t imagine life without it. I don’t want to risk proving it was only ever just a dream. But I think the world I imagined deserves a chance to exist.

And not everyone is going to want to be part of it. That’s okay. Three conversations that don’t click don’t tell me what the next three will bring. You don’t need permission to pursue your dreams. Saying them out loud brings them into the real world. It’s also the only way you’ll ever find someone else that wants to dream with you.

Dreaming together doesn’t make the other hard parts disappear. There’s still money to find, time to make, things to learn, and problems to solve. Not knowing how to do something isn’t proof it can’t be done. And you don’t have to figure it all out alone.

My dream is to live in a world where more people get to see their dreams come true. I’m building software that I think could help people accomplish things together that they couldn’t accomplish alone.

I don’t know what your dreams are. Maybe something you want to build, a way to improve your community, or something you’ve always wanted to try. I’d love to hear them all! And if there’s something I can do to help, I’d love to try.
